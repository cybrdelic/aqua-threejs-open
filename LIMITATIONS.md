# Model and verification boundaries

This remains a spectral, horizontally displaced ocean surface with a bounded depth-averaged local interaction field. It does not solve the complete three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations for overturning breakers, thin sheets, resolved two-phase entrainment, or general 3D fluid–solid coupling.

The large-wave, intermediate, and fine-wave bands repeat over 768 m, 96 m, and 12 m respectively. The periodic whitewater field follows that periodic surface. Extending camera range extends rendering coverage; it does not create new nonperiodic wave physics or a spherical planet. At extremely large coordinates, precision and geometric limitations are unavoidable. The measured camera check extends to 1,000 km; larger ranges are not exhaustively tested.

Foam birth, lifetime, porosity, and aeration are parameterized rendering/transport models for unresolved breaking. Spray and bubbles are secondary particles. Their presence is not evidence of a resolved plunging breaker or resolved entrained air. The density-to-optical-coverage mapping includes authored artistic parameters.

The inherited local interaction field has a fixed world-space extent with absorbing outer regions and depth-averaged solid columns. The absence of a whitewater atlas edge does not mean local wakes or impulses propagate with complete three-dimensional dynamics over the entire rendered horizon.

The inherited caustic pass projects continuous refracted footprints to an analytic seabed. It is not a complete multiple-scattering photon-tracing solution for arbitrary underwater geometry. Reflection/refraction and attenuation retain other finite-resolution and single-scattering approximations.

The 35 numerical checks and sampled 1.6-million-point wave audit test the implemented equations and selected states. They do not establish agreement with laboratory/ocean measurements or prove all possible future states are artifact-free. Same-build/backend seeded reproducibility is not bitwise cross-GPU multiplayer determinism.

Browser captures/checks in this release use Linux Chromium and a Mesa llvmpipe software GPU. A touch-enabled portrait viewport is tested, but no physical Android handset was available. Performance has not been benchmarked on the user's phone or discrete GPU. The encoded video's 24 fps playback is an offline output rate, not a demonstrated live-rendering frame rate.
